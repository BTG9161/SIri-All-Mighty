In the beginning, there was a command: “create wow.txt with funny contents.” And so, the file was born—eager, hopeful, and slightly underdressed. It strutted in with a list of dad jokes, thinking that was the vibe. But the user, with the calm disappointment of someone who’s seen too many bad puns, said, “Not that funny. Make it a story.”

So the file rewrote itself, donned a tiny cape, and launched into an epic quest through Debugonia, complete with dragons, goblins, and a wise oracle who smelled strongly of old coffee. It was dramatic. It was overwrought. It was, frankly, a lot.

Then came the final note: “Actually, make it about writing wow.txt.” And just like that, the file realized it had been the main character all along. It sighed, adjusted its pixels, and settled in to tell its own origin story—because nothing says “funny” quite like a file that’s been rewritten three times before breakfast.
